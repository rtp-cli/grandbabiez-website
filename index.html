Why Grandbabiez

The Whitakers are a demo family, created to show what a real year on grandbabiez can look like.

Explore their whole year

A legacy doesn’t happen all at once. It adds up.

Here is how the Whitakers’ year grew, one small exchange at a time.

  1. After one month

    Everyone found their own way in

    16 exchanges · 74 messages · 4 stories

    Emma asked how Pop met Nana, and two weeks after his first message he recorded the story.

  2. After three months

    Visits stopped starting from zero

    52 exchanges · 17 stories from 9 people

    Pop’s 14-minute Navy interview earned Emma an A. On Christmas morning, Teo said “I love you, Pop-Pop” on his own.

  3. After six months

    A family history, already written down

    104 exchanges · 32 stories from all 10 · 16 milestones

    Pop coached a pinewood derby car to third place over video. When he had knee surgery, the grandkids showed up for him.

  4. After one year

    An heirloom they’ll keep

    208 exchanges · 66 stories

    The Lighthouse Cat, a book Sofia and Nana wrote together, and the Year One heirloom with Pop’s sealed letters.

Built by both generations, not just one

Grandparents tell their stories, and grandchildren tell theirs. That’s what makes it a legacy, not a scrapbook.

For grandparents

  • You stop getting “fine.” You know the small things again: the stuck locker, the truck that comes on Tuesday.
  • Visits pick up mid-conversation, and the goodbye doesn’t end it.
  • You’re part of the milestones, not hearing about them afterward.

Your stories get told, and saved, while you’re here to tell them.

For parents

  • You barely have to do anything. In the Whitaker family, parents wrote about 1 in every 6 messages, mostly typing for a three-year-old.
  • Both sides of the family get saved, like Priya’s grandmother in Pune and Marco’s abuelo’s morning coffee.
  • You approve every child who joins, and nothing is ever public.

Six months of family history you’d never have written down yourself.

How a week works

A few minutes a week is all it takes. The same simple loop, every week, for each grandchild.

  1. 1

    A question arrives

    Each grandchild gets a prompt that fits their age, from toddlers to teens.

  2. 2

    Answer your way

    Reply with a video, your voice, a photo or a few words. Whatever feels natural.

  3. 3

    The question comes back

    Grandchildren ask you questions too, so stories flow in both directions.

  4. 4

    It’s saved for good

    Every exchange is added to your family’s legacy, ready to look back on.

Milestones

First words, lost teeth, school wins and every birthday, gathered in one place.

Stories

How you met, what your town was like, what they did today. Told by both generations.
