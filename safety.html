For parents

A parent is in charge, start to finish.

Grandbabiez connects a grandparent with a grandchild. A parent decides whether that happens, who is in the circle, and what their child can see and share — and can change their mind at any time.

How a circle starts

Nothing happens to your child until you have said yes. The order is deliberate:

  • ✓A grandparent signs up and invites the family by phone number.
  • ✓You receive a direct notice explaining exactly what would be collected and why.
  • ✓You give — or refuse — verifiable consent. Nothing about your child is collected before this point.
  • ✓You approve who is in the circle. Up to ten people, all added by the family.
  • ✓You choose what your child can see and share.

The note you receive at step two is reproduced word for word on the parent notice page, so you can read it before anyone asks you anything.

What you control

Who is in the circle

Every member is invited by the family and approved by you. There is no way for someone to request access, stumble across your child, or be recommended into the circle. Ten people is the ceiling, not a starting point.

What your child can share

You decide whether your child can send photos, voice notes, or video, and you can change that at any time. If you would rather the answer stayed at written replies and drawings, that is a setting, not a special request.

Whether it continues

You can pause a circle or withdraw consent entirely, with one tap or one phone call. Withdrawing consent stops collection and deletes what we hold about your child. We will not ask you to justify it.

What your child never gets

  • —A public profile, a feed, or anything discoverable by strangers.
  • —Messages from anyone outside the circle you approved.
  • —Advertising, sponsored content, or product recommendations.
  • —Open-ended content nobody vetted. Every prompt goes to the grandparent first. They read it, and nothing reaches your grandchild unless they choose to send it. Prompts are shaped for your grandchild’s age, and we keep a reviewed set that we fall back on.
  • —A record that follows them. Nothing is sold, and nothing is public.

The design goal is narrow on purpose: one small ritual a week between a child and a grandparent who loves them, and nothing else riding along with it.

COPPA, briefly

The Children’s Online Privacy Protection Act sets the rules for collecting information from children under 13 in the United States. It requires verifiable parental consent before collection, a clear notice of what is collected, and a way for parents to review and delete it.

We are building to follow COPPA and treat it as the floor rather than the ceiling. We are also evaluating an independent COPPA Safe Harbor certification, so that somebody outside this company is checking our work.

The full detail is in our Privacy Policy.
