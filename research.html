The science

Why staying close is worth the effort.

We didn’t invent the idea that grandparents matter. Decades of university research — across the United States, Europe, and beyond — find that a warm grandparent–grandchild bond is good for both generations. That evidence is why we’re building Grandbabiez. Here’s an honest, plain-language summary, with a link to every study so you can read the original.

How to read this page

These studies are about grandparent–grandchild relationships in general — not about Grandbabiez, which is new and hasn’t been studied. We’re not claiming an app produces these results. What the research does show, again and again, is two things that happen to be exactly what we’re designing for: the benefits come from light, warm, voluntary connection, and emotional closeness matters more than sheer frequency of contact. A gentle weekly moment is the light, warm end of that spectrum.

What it does for grandchildren

A warm grandparent is good for a child.

The clearest, most repeated findings are about kindness and resilience — and they reach further into life than you might expect.

More kindness, fewer behavior problems

Across studies in the US, South Africa, and Israel, children close to an involved grandparent showed more prosocial behavior and fewer conduct, aggression, and hyperactivity problems — often over and above closeness to their own parents.

A buffer when life gets hard

Grandparents matter most under adversity — family conflict, a parent’s struggles, distance. A UK study found a grandmother’s involvement measurably softened the effect of hard early experiences on a child’s wellbeing.

The warmth lasts into adulthood

A 2025 Stanford study found that grandparent support in childhood predicted greater emotional wellbeing at ages 18–29 — and the link held even after accounting for closeness to parents, and even when the grandparent had since died.

Closeness beats frequency

It isn’t about how often you talk. Research consistently finds it’s the emotional closeness of the bond — not the number of calls — that carries the benefit, and it works best alongside a child’s bond with their parents, never as a replacement.

What it does for grandparents

And it gives back to you, too.

The research on grandparents’ own health is striking — with one important condition we’re honest about below.

Linked to a longer life

In a long-running German study, grandparents who helped with grandchildren occasionally — not full-time — were markedly less likely to die over the follow-up, with roughly double the median survival of non-helpers.

A sharper mind

Using a design built to test cause and effect, one large European study found that caring for grandchildren actually improved grandparents’ verbal fluency — and other work links regular, light involvement to better thinking skills years later.

Less depression, more life satisfaction

Across tens of thousands of older Europeans, light grandchild involvement is tied to fewer depressive symptoms, higher life satisfaction — and grandparents who feel younger than their years.

Less loneliness

A 2025 study of nearly 31,000 grandparents found that any level of grandchild involvement lowered the risk of loneliness — with steady, ongoing connection the most protective of all.

A few of the studies behind this.

A selection from the roughly forty peer-reviewed studies we reviewed. Every link goes to the original publication.

For grandchildren

Grandparent support in childhood predicted emotional wellbeing at ages 18–29 — even after a grandparent had died.

Stephenson & Carstensen, 2025 · Stanford University · Frontiers in Psychology ›

A maternal grandmother’s involvement measurably buffered the effect of adverse early experiences on children’s wellbeing.

Helle et al., 2024 · University of Turku · Scientific Reports ›

Across a meta-analysis of 20 studies, grandparental warmth was linked to less depression and greater resilience in children.

Zhao et al., 2025 · Nanjing Normal University · Behavioral Sciences ›

Emotional closeness — not frequency of contact — was what reduced depressive symptoms; more contact alone did not.

For grandparents

Grandparents who provided occasional, non-custodial care had about 37% lower mortality risk — roughly double the median survival of non-caregivers.

Hilbrand et al., 2017 · University of Basel / Max Planck Institute · Evolution & Human Behavior ›

Using a cause-and-effect design, caring for grandchildren improved grandparents’ verbal fluency.

Arpino & Bordone, 2014 · Universitat Pompeu Fabra / IIASA · Journal of Marriage and Family ›

Any level of grandchild involvement lowered loneliness risk among nearly 31,000 grandparents; steady connection helped most.

Juma et al., 2025 · University of the Basque Country · Archives of Gerontology and Geriatrics ›

A systematic review found most studies of light, non-coresident grandparenting reported better grandparent health — while custodial care showed the opposite.

Danielsbacka et al., 2022 · University of Turku · European Journal of Ageing ›

A note on how we read the evidence

We’ve tried to represent this literature fairly, including its limits. A few things worth knowing:

  • Most of these studies show associations, not proven cause and effect. The strongest evidence comes from the studies that followed the same families over time.
  • The benefits are consistently tied to emotional closeness and light involvement — not to intensive or custodial caregiving, which the research links to worse outcomes for grandparents.
  • Physical-health outcomes are under-studied; most of this work measures mental health and social-emotional wellbeing.
  • Grandbabiez itself has not been studied. We’re building it because of this evidence, and our founding families will help us learn what actually works.
